6.1 The problem: zeros in small populations

Between late childhood and middle age, the annual probability of dying is of the order of \(10^{-4}\) to \(10^{-3}\). In a population the size of Iceland or Luxembourg that means a handful of deaths — or none — at each single age in a given year. When the HMD records no deaths, \(m_x\) and \(q_x\) are exactly zero, and their logit is \(-\infty\) (in practice, the floor \(\operatorname{logit}(10^{-8}) \approx -18.4\), far below any real value near \(-7\)). Left in the tensor, such cells are enormous outliers that the decomposition would spend rank on.

6.2 The remedy: pool adjacent years

Following Clark (2001), we pool adjacent years: deaths and exposures are summed over a block of years and the pooled rate \(m_x = \sum_t D_{x,t} / \sum_t E_{x,t}\) replaces the single-year rates. A block is just wide enough to contain at least one death at every affected age, so large populations are not pooled at all and small ones only as much as they need. The algorithm, implemented in mdmx.data.adaptive_pooling(), is:

  1. Valley ages. Ages whose median \(q_x\) across all populations, sexes and years is below \(q_{\text{thresh}} =\) 0.005.
  2. Flag. A population–sex is pooled only if one of its non-exceptional years has \(m_x = 0\) at a valley age. Its pooling ages are the valley ages plus any other age below 80 at which it has a zero rate (in the very smallest populations zeros also occur a few years above the valley). Above that age the HMD’s rates are smoothed by a model and are never zero.
  3. Blocks. The flagged population–sex’s non-exceptional years are taken in calendar order and split into runs wherever more than 3 calendar years are missing (so 2019 and 2023 belong to the same run across the COVID years 2020–22, but a long gap breaks the run). Within a run, years are added to a block until every pooling age has at least one death in the block; the block is then closed and the next begins. Years left over at the end of a run join the previous block.
  4. Pool and rebuild. In a block of two or more years, the pooled rate \(\sum_t D_{x,t}/\sum_t E_{x,t}\) is computed at each pooling age, and in every year of the block \(q_x\) at those ages is recomputed from the pooled rate and that year’s own \(a_x\): \(q_x = m_x / \{1 + (1-a_x)\,m_x\}\).
  5. Everything else is untouched. Outside the pooling ages, every year keeps the HMD’s published \(q_x\); single-year blocks (years that already have deaths at every pooling age) and unflagged populations are not changed at all; and exceptional years are never pooled.

How much noise does pooling remove? A good measure is the year-to-year change in \(\operatorname{logit}(q_x)\) at each age: real mortality changes slowly, so large jumps between consecutive years are mostly noise. The figure compares the median absolute year-to-year change, before and after pooling, over the same country-years (all non-exceptional years of the pooled populations, zero-free cells only so that the floor does not dominate the “before” curve).

Code 
pooled_ps = set(map(tuple, blocks[["pop", "sex"]].drop_duplicates().to_numpy()))
nonexc = labels.loc[labels["label"] == 0, ["pop", "year"]]
def yearly_change(lt):
    df = lt.merge(nonexc, on=["pop", "year"])
    df = df[[ (p, s) in pooled_ps for p, s in zip(df["pop"], df["sex"])]].copy()
    df["z"] = lifetable.logit(df["qx"], P.data.q_min)
    df = df.sort_values(["pop", "sex", "age", "year"])
    g = df.groupby(["pop", "sex", "age"], observed=True)
    df["dz"] = g["z"].diff().abs()
    df["consecutive"] = g["year"].diff() == 1
    return df.set_index(["pop", "sex", "age", "year"])
before, after = yearly_change(lt_cur), yearly_change(lt_pool)
ok = before["consecutive"] & (before["qx"] > 0) & (before.groupby(level=[0, 1, 2]).shift(1)["qx"] > 0)
med_before = before.loc[ok, "dz"].groupby(level="age").median()
med_after = after.loc[ok[ok].index, "dz"].groupby(level="age").median()

ax.axvspan(valley.min() - 0.5, valley.max() + 0.5, color="#fdae61", alpha=0.2, label="Valley ages")
ax.set_xlabel("Age"); ax.set_ylabel(r"median |Δ logit$(q_x)$| between consecutive years")
ax.legend()
plotting.save_figure(fig, "fig-s2-smoothing-by-age")
plt.show()
valley_mask = med_before.index.isin(valley)
reduction = 1 - med_after[valley_mask].mean() / med_before[valley_mask].mean()
latex.report("dataPoolNoiseReduction", 100 * reduction, digits=0,
             desc="Percent reduction in median year-to-year |Δlogit q| at valley ages, pooled populations")
print(f"Year-to-year noise at valley ages reduced by {100 * reduction:.0f}%")
